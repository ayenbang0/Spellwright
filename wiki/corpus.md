### Area Boost  [Rarity: Rare, Spell, Spell Type: Spell Passive] (2025-07-16)
{{Spell InfoBox|image=Area_Boost.png|type=Spell Passive|rarity=Rare|misc_a_1=Copies Level 1|misc_a_2=Copies Level 2|misc_a_3=Copies Level 3}}

(this) is a Rare Spell of Type: Spell Passive. It effectively is a rather advanced variant of the Mimicry Cube.

(this) takes up 4 slots to equip into a wand, then the first spell that is placed to the right of it will be duplicated into every other wand you are holding, in the leftmost position, including the "host" wand using (this). This will not consume a slot in those wands, the effect is created in a new, free, locked slot; correspondingly, copying a spell that needs something else to the left of it to work such as Echo or Duet is not recommended, unless you use (this) twice to load a triggering spell and then one of those two.

* Note, the copied spell is put to the left of any already existing locked slots on a wand, such as the Soul Set's natural Wand Spirits.
* If (this) is fit twice into two separate wands, the copied spell in the higher slot will go first (i.e. a copied spell set in wand 2 will take effect first before a copied spell in wand 3)

Like with Mimicry Cube, the level of (this) determines up to what level of the copied spell it will duplicate, and a high level (this) cannot create a higher level copy of a low level spell.

SPECIAL INTERACTIONS
* Magic Vine: Is never considered to have the stacking effect from empty slots to the right of it, so will always be one single iteration of the buff.
* Multi-slot spells like Arcane Barrier or Core of Flame: Do not take up their extra slots, they only show 1 single slot at the beginning of the wand but the effects are still at full strength.
* Capacity Expansion Stone: Extra slots are still added to the far right end of the wands as normal.

### Chests  [] (2024-12-28)
Lost Chests are scattered around the world outside the camp. Often simply in plain sight, but other times hidden behind walls or over spike pits. They can also appear in Side Rooms, such as the Cursed Chest Room. Chests come in a variety of forms, and most require some sort of sacrifice to unlock.

== Chest Types ==

=== Cursed Chests ===
Require the hero to accept Curses in order to open the chest. The hero can usually tell which curse they will have to accept before they open it, though some Curses and effects can render them unreadable.  Cursed Chests often appear in Side Rooms, where many can appear at once.

=== Locked Chests ===
The most common type; locked chests require a key to open, and are usually in plain sight.

=== Spike Chests ===
Spike Chests are another common type of chest, and require a sacrifice of health (10 hp) to open. They can kill the player.

=== Unlocked Chests ===
A rare type that, when it appears, is almost always hidden.

### Damage Calculation  [] (2025-02-15)
This page is work in progress, info may be incomplete

Damage formula

Here is how damage is calculated in Magicraft:

(((Spell base damage  + Base damage increase) × Spell damage multiplier + Mirrored damage) 

   × Final damage multiplier 

   × Conditional final damage 

   + Bubble growth damage) 

       × Crit multiplier × Enemy damage taken de-buff

Base damage: Damage a spell does when hitting an enemy when unmodified.

Base damage increase: Base damage increase from things like Enchanting Coin from coins, Fierce Dragon Breath over time, Supernova from charging, and Blast Surge only if spell has a radius, Dimension Walker off of current mana.

Spell damage multiplier: Changed by Relics (additive), Curses (additive), spells like DMG Enhanced (additive), Overload (additive), Energizer wand (additive) when criteria is met, Reflection (multiplicative) and Energy Saving Mode (multiplicative). Total curse + relics damage can be seen on the character screen in the pause menu.

Mirrored damage: That's the damage that Duet gives to the payload. Most spells marked with (DPS) in the tool tip will divide the Mirrored damage over their Ticks of damage, except for Skull of the Cthulhu for some reason it gets x1.75 Duet damage for all ticks.

Final damage: Multiplier affected by Capacity Expansion Stone (multiplicative), Wand stat of final damage (multiplicative), Thunderstorm when meeting condition of hitting a single enemy (multiplicative). 

Conditional final damage multiplier: Affected by Shining Star Arrow with crit condition met and Skull of the Cthulhu scaling from max MP. Formula for Skull of the Cthulhu damage multiplier from mp is: 1 + Max mp × 0.001/0.002 (for skull+/skull++ respectively). Why separate it from Final damage? Because, unlike Final Damage, the Conditional final damage multiplier will be applied when calculating the damage a spell will supply to spells after Duet. Skull of the Cthulhu mp to damage multiplier also gets recalculated when Fusion Summoning with more skulls. 

Bubble growth damage: Condensed Water Bubble is a weird spell in that it's damage gain from lifetime is not affected by multiplicative damage modifications. The formula for Bubble growth damage is: Lifetime in seconds x 0.2/0.25/0.35 depending on bubble level x 100/170/290 base damage x Spell damage multiplier without the Energy Saving Mode.

Crit multiplier: Applied if the roll for a crit succeeds, the multiplier is x2 by default is able to upgraded with Blade of Fury.

Enemy damage taken de-buff: De-buff applied by Magic Bullet making enemies take x1.5/x2 damage when hitting them with bullet+ and bullet++ respectively.

About how duet takes damage from the spell on the left

Resulting mirrored damage that a spell will provide when using Duet is calculated as followed: 

(((Spell base damage  + Base damage increase) × Spell damage multiplier) + Mirrored damage + Bubble growth damage) × Conditional final damage multiplier × 0.3/0.6/1.2 (depending on Duet level)

Note: In duet calculation spell base damage for most dps spells is its total damage over a second (the number on the tool-tip), except Skull of the Cthulhu for which it takes its one tick of damage number.

Note: Magicraft rounds a lot, after like every single math operation... that means that the order of the multipliers (and potentially even the order of the separate sources of multipliers)  being applied matters. I am not figuring it out, good luck.😁

{{Spell InfoBox|image=Duet.png|type=Spell Boost|rarity=Normal|misc_a_1=Casts the spell on the right at the completion of the spell on the left|misc_b_1=The spell on the right inherits 30% of the damage from the spell on the left|misc_a_2=Casts the spell on the right at the completion of the spell on the left|misc_b_2=The spell on the right inherits 60% of the damage from the spell on the left|misc_a_3=Casts the spell on the right at the completion of the spell on the left|misc_b_3=The spell on the right inherits 120% of the damage from the spell on the left}}(this) is a Normal Spell of Type: Spell Boost. Even though the spell description does not mention it, the damage bonus from Duet applies to all spells inside the payload of duet, except those separated by another Trigger spell. 

Example 1:

Wand with simultaneous casting 1

Meteor + Duet + Volley + Magic Bullet + Laser

The duet damage bonus will apply to both the magic bullet and the laser. 

Example 2:

Wand with simultaneous casting 2

Meteor + Duet + Magic Bullet + Dazzling Fireworks + Laser + Butterfly + Rainbow

The duet damage bonus is applied to the magic bullet and rainbow but not to laser and butterfly, because those are inside of trigger spell payload.

DPS spells and Duet: When applying the duet damage to spells that do damage over time (those that have "DPS" in brackets) the Duet damage bonus is added to the overall damage per second. This means you will be hitting the enemy for dps divided by the Tick rate of the spell for each tick. 

Exception to this rule is Skull of the Cthulhu which gains x1.75 Duet damage on all its ticks.

Lightning dash does damage multiple times but does not have a DPS tag, so it benefits normally from the duet damage boost.

### Mana Absorption  [Rarity: Epic, Spell, Spell Type: Spell Projectiles] (2026-01-22)
{{Spell InfoBox|image=Mana_Absorption.png|desc=Legendary mana-absorbing spell|type=Spell Projectiles|rarity=Epic|mana_cost1=8|dmg1=24|cd1=-0.2s|crit_rate1=40%|misc_a_1=Restores the mana spent for the casting wand when hitting an enemy for the first time|misc_b_1=Unable to craft}}(this) is an Epic Spell of Type: Spell Projectiles. While this spell doesn't do much damage or change with upgrades, it can provide a steady flow of mana to the wand casting it.

Description: Returns mana to you after completing its mission gratefully, but if you can cast it with less MP, what a deal

How to get free mana: Mana Absorption is not only useful for shooting itself for free; it can also be used to generate more mana than you spend, allowing you to sustain larger mana costs from other spells. The way the spell typically works is by dividing the mana returned among the amount of projectiles released. This means you can't simply put Split or Multi-Shot before it to gain free mana. Instead, you should increase the number of spells that will release Mana Absorption, or use spells that will release multiple Mana Absorptions themselves.

Here are some builds that will generate free mana:

* Arcane Nova + Mana Absorption 
* Multi-Shot + any projectile spell + any Trigger spell + Mana Absorption
* Multi-Shot + Split + Mana Absorption 
* Any projectile spell + Dazzling Fireworks + Mana Absorption

### Relics  [] (2025-07-08)
'Relics' are items gained from Relic Rooms (and random chest drops) that grant you special powers. Relics are the opposite of Curses, though some can still have negative effects. Like Spells, Relics are lost on Death. However, unlike Spells, Relics are entirely passive & cannot be dropped or removed. Relics can, however, be upgraded or re-rolled by Potions or other Relics.

If you defeat the boss of a level without taking any HP damage (you can still lose shields), the game will open another room before proceeding with Crimsom Relics. You will have the chance to get 3 special Epic Relics, each one at the cost of decreasing your maximum HP by an amount .

While playing on Nightmare 2, there is a 1% chance for a relic to be Epic. On Nightmare 3, this chance is 1.6%.

### Side Room  [] (2024-03-07)
Side Rooms are rarely-encountered areas that adjoin both normal and non-combat rooms. Siderooms in combat areas are usually locked and require a Key, or other special circumstances. Some Relics & effects can guarantee certain Side Rooms.

==Combat Area Side Rooms==
These rooms are always locked or sealed in some way, and generally either require a key or a special event to open. Most 'Combat Area' Side Rooms are puzzles, assault courses, or otherwise challenges to the player. Combat Area Side Rooms are the most common form of Side Room you will encounter.  These include:

* Rooms full of Cursed Chests.
* Spinning Fire Wheel Rooms
* Assault Courses
* Dark Room Assault Courses
* Puzzle Button Rooms
* Memory Sequence Rooms

== Shop & WitchWorks Side Rooms ==
Sometimes the Shop and WitchWorks contain various Side Rooms, which can appear on the left or right of the main area. These include:

* The Potion Shop.
* The Rune Circle.

== Boss Area Side Rooms ==
The rarest and most dangerous Side Rooms are the secret Blood Rooms that only adjoin certain (randomly chosen) Bosses. These can only be accessed by defeating the bosses without taking damage. At the current time, these are the only known Boss Monster side rooms.

Penetration

Penetration allows other spells to hit a target multiple times, but the spell needs to re-enter the enemy hit-box to be able to hit the same target again. Lightning Dash and Boomerang Blade are special in that they have penetration, but can also hit the same target multiple times without leaving their hit-box. However the tick-rate when doing so is relatively slow at only 3 hits per second. What you can do is exploit the "leave the hit-box to hit again" mechanic by either Tracking the edge of the enemy hit-box or increasing their speed with Accelerator making them shake in and out of the enemy hit-boxes rapidly.
